# contradiction

A contradiction is two accounts of the same thing that do not match.
Those accounts may come from one speaker at different times, from different people, or from a speaker set against the documents.

The work starts by collecting assertions of fact.
An assertion is about an event, a condition, or a person's conduct.
Each assertion is copied verbatim, with its page.
Opinions, legal argument, and pleasantries stay out.

Each assertion notes who said it, which event it concerns, the date they give, and whether they affirm it or deny it.
A short neutral restatement is for grouping only.
The citation is the verbatim span, not the restatement.
The speaker is the person who made the assertion.
When a report quotes someone, the speaker is the person quoted, not the author of the report.
One event keeps one name.
An event that an earlier reading already named keeps that name.

The accounts of one event are then set side by side.
Agreement is recorded as well as conflict.
Agreement is part of the evidence.

A conflict is one of three kinds.

- Internal means the same person, in different documents or on different dates.
- Between parties means different people.
- Against the record means a claim set against a document that is not that person's own account.

A conflict is central when the user, or the question being asked, names that event as one the case turns on.
Any other conflict is peripheral.

A missing date, or anything else that blocks the comparison, is a gap.
It is listed, and not forced into a kind.

People's accounts of the same event vary in small details.
Accounts also change as memory fades.
A contradiction on its own is not a finding that someone was untruthful.
The conflict is reported.
Weighing it is a later step.
